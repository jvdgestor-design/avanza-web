Italian pension and living in Spain: where it is taxed and how it is declared

Esta guía en español →

If you are tax resident in Spain and receive a pension from Italy, the tax treaty between the two countries decides which of them taxes it: a pension for past employment is taxed in Spain, and an Italian public service pension is, in general, taxed in Italy. Grupo Avanza Consultores, tax and accounting adviser in Paterna (Valencia), checks which pensions you receive and who pays them, and declares them in your Spanish income tax return. We work remotely across Spain and reply in written English by WhatsApp or email, with a quote before we start.

Pensions for past employment: taxed in Spain

Pensions and other similar remuneration paid to a resident of a State in consideration of past employment may be taxed only in that State, except for the public pensions covered by article 19.2 (article 18 of the Spain–Italy double taxation convention, in force since 14 November 1980). If you are resident in Spain, your Italian pension for past private-sector employment is taxed here and not in Italy, according to the Spanish Tax Agency’s leaflet on Italy.

Italian public service pensions

Pensions paid by the Italian State or its political or administrative subdivisions or local authorities, either directly or out of funds created for the purpose, for services rendered to them are taxable only in Italy (article 19.2.a); in Spain they are exempt, with progressivity, according to the Spanish Tax Agency’s leaflet. They are taxable only in Spain if the pensioner lives in Spain and holds Spanish nationality (article 19.2.b); the convention does not require Spanish nationality alone: holding Spanish nationality is enough (articles 19.2.b and 3.1.h). Those paid for services rendered in connection with a commercial or industrial activity of those bodies follow article 18 (article 19.3). Where the pension is exempt in Spain, Spain may take it into account when calculating the tax on the rest of your income (article 22.4).

How it is declared in Spain

Pensions from public social security schemes and other public retirement benefits are declared as employment income (article 17.2.a of the Spanish Personal Income Tax Act); for private pensions, the classification depends on the type of scheme and is checked against its documents. Because the Italian payer is not obliged to withhold Spanish tax, according to the Spanish Tax Agency’s leaflet, the employment income threshold that triggers the duty to file falls from 22,000 to 15,876 euros (article 96.2.a and 3.c); a pension that is exempt in Spain is not taken into account for the duty to file (same leaflet). For income that Italy may tax under the convention, Spain allows a deduction for the tax paid there, not exceeding the part of the Spanish tax attributable to that income (article 22.3 of the convention and article 80 of the Act). Anyone entitled to that deduction must file in any case (article 96.4).

Frequently asked questions

About your Italian pension

Is my Italian pension taxed in Spain or in Italy?

If it is for past employment and you are tax resident in Spain, in Spain: such pensions may be taxed only in the State of residence (article 18 of the Spain–Italy convention). Italian public service pensions are, in general, taxed in Italy (article 19.2).

I hold Spanish nationality. Where is my Italian public service pension taxed?

If you live in Spain and hold Spanish nationality, in Spain: those pensions may be taxed only in the State of residence where the beneficiary holds the nationality of that State (article 19.2.b of the convention).

Do I have to file a Spanish income tax return?

As a general rule, yes, if your employment income, pension included, exceeds 15,876 euros and the payer is not obliged to withhold Spanish tax (article 96.3.c of the Personal Income Tax Act); a pension exempt in Spain does not count towards that threshold, according to the Spanish Tax Agency’s leaflet. Yes, in any case, if you are entitled to the international double taxation deduction (article 96.4).

Does the exempt pension count for the tax on my other income?

It may. Where income is exempt in Spain under the convention, Spain may take it into account when calculating the tax on the rest of your income (article 22.4 of the convention).

Renewing an employed residence permit: what decides your Spanish income tax

Español

Renewing your residence and work authorisation does not change your taxes: whether you are tax resident is decided by article 9 of the Spanish Personal Income Tax Act (IRPF), and whether you must file a return is decided by article 96. A change of employer or a period on unemployment benefit during the year usually means more than one payer; if the amounts from the second and subsequent payers exceed €1,500, the filing threshold drops from €22,000 to €15,876.

The renewal in brief

Temporary residence and work authorisations for employment (cuenta ajena) under the general regime are renewed under articles 80 and 81 of Royal Decree 1155/2024. The application is filed during the two months before expiry, and filing within that period extends the previous authorisation until a decision is made. Article 80.1 also provides for the three months after expiry, with the same extension, although late filing may lead to penalty proceedings.

Continuing in the same job is one renewal route, but not the only one: article 80 covers other situations involving work completed, a new contract or certain benefits. Under article 81.1 the renewal is for four years, unless long-term residence applies; if the initial authorisation lasted less than one year and one of the situations in article 80 applies, it is renewed for the duration of the activity, up to a maximum of one year.

Once the renewal is granted, the TIE card must be requested in person within one month of notification (article 81.2). The authorisation, the card and your taxes are three separate matters: what follows is the tax side.

Do you have to file a return? What decides it

One payer

If your only income is from employment and it does not exceed €22,000 in the year, you are not required to file, unless another situation in article 96 applies.

IRPF Act (Law 35/2006), article 96.

Two or more payers

The threshold drops to €15,876 when there is more than one payer and the amounts from the second and subsequent payers exceed €1,500 in total. Changing employer mid-year or receiving unemployment benefit means more than one payer; what then counts is whether the amounts from the second and subsequent payers exceed €1,500.

IRPF Act (Law 35/2006), article 96.

You always file

Whatever you earn, you must file if you were registered as self-employed at any point in the year or are entitled to the international double taxation deduction.

IRPF Act (Law 35/2006), article 96.

Your permit does not decide your tax residence

Holding a renewed permit or a valid TIE does not make you tax resident, and not holding one does not exclude you. Under article 9.1 of the IRPF Act you are resident if you spend more than 183 days of the calendar year in Spain, counting occasional absences unless you prove tax residence in another country, or if the main centre or base of your activities or economic interests is in Spain. Residence is also presumed, unless proven otherwise, if your spouse (not legally separated) and dependent minor children live in Spain. If another country also treats you as resident, the double taxation treaty decides. Our guide to tax residence when moving to Spain explains this.

For the Tax Agency, your tax number is your NIE (article 20 of Royal Decree 1065/2007); if you do not have one yet, the Tax Agency assigns a tax number (NIF) starting with M.

If you have income or assets outside Spain

As a Spanish tax resident you are taxed in Spain on all your income, wherever it arises and wherever the payer is based (article 2 of the IRPF Act): rent from a property back home, interest on an account there or a salary paid abroad for part of the year. If that income has already been taxed in the other country, the international double taxation deduction prevents you paying twice, but being entitled to it means you must file a return even if your Spanish salary is below the threshold.

Accounts, securities, insurance and property abroad may also require the Form 720 information return (eighteenth additional provision of the General Tax Act). Virtual currencies held in custody abroad are reported separately, on Form 721. The thresholds and deadline are in that guide.

The inbound worker regime: a deadline that does not come back

If you moved to Spain with an employment contract and had not been tax resident in the previous five tax years, the special regime for inbound workers (article 93 of the IRPF Act, the "Beckham Law") lets you pay 24% on taxable income up to €600,000, which includes employment income, in the year you become resident and the following five. You opt by notifying the Tax Agency within six months of the start of the activity shown in your Social Security registration (article 116 of the IRPF Regulations). The period runs from the start of the activity shown in your Social Security registration, not from the renewal: check that date. It is a maximum period (article 116.1.a of the IRPF Regulations): once the six months have passed, the option can no longer be notified.

Family abroad and joint returns

A joint return is only possible for the family unit defined in articles 82 and 83 of the IRPF Act, as made up on 31 December, and only if all its members are Spanish income taxpayers. If your spouse or children live abroad and are not Spanish tax residents, as a rule you file individually.

Unfiled years

Looking back from the year of a renewal, it is common to find that an earlier year had two payers, income abroad or a pending Form 720. The order matters: first establish in which years you were tax resident and required to file, then file the missing returns. See our guide to regularising your taxes in Spain as a foreigner.

Tax questions for employees in Spain

Does renewing my residence permit make me a Spanish tax resident?

No. Tax residence does not depend on the authorisation or the TIE, but on article 9.1 of the IRPF Act: more than 183 days in Spain in the calendar year, or the main centre of your activities or economic interests here. If another country also treats you as resident, the double taxation treaty decides.

I changed employer or received unemployment benefit. Do I have to file?

It depends on the total. With more than one payer, if the amounts from the second and subsequent payers exceed €1,500, the threshold for not filing drops from €22,000 to €15,876 of employment income (article 96 of the IRPF Act). Above that figure, you file.

Can I opt for the Beckham Law when I renew my permit?

Only if you meet the requirements of article 93 of the IRPF Act and notify the option in time. The period runs from the start of the activity shown in your Social Security registration, not from the renewal: check that date. It is a maximum period (article 116.1.a of the IRPF Regulations): once the six months have passed, the option can no longer be notified.

Do I have to declare in Spain what I earn in my home country?

If you are a Spanish tax resident, yes: all your income is taxed, wherever it arises (article 2 of the IRPF Act). If you have already paid tax there, the international double taxation deduction corrects it, and being entitled to it means you must file a return.

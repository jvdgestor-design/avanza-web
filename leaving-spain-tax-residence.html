Leaving Spain: what happens with Spanish tax when you stop being resident

Esta guía en español → · Эта статья на русском →

If you are leaving Spain to live in another country, your last year here and whatever you keep in Spain have their own tax rules. Grupo Avanza Consultores, tax and accounting adviser in Paterna (Valencia), checks in which year you stop being resident, prepares your last income tax return with what has to be included in it and, afterwards, the non-resident returns for what you still have here. The year you leave is usually assessed as a whole

The Spanish income tax period is the calendar year (article 12.1 of the Personal Income Tax Act) and residence is assessed over that year (article 9.1). Someone who leaves mid-year can remain resident for the whole year after spending more than 183 days here, counting sporadic absences unless tax residence in another country is proved, or if the main core or base of their activities or economic interests is here, and then declares worldwide income for the whole year in Spain. If the destination country also treats you as resident that year, the tax treaty between the two, if there is one, decides, and it has to be checked against your dates. The date on the plane ticket does not decide this on its own.

What goes into your last return

When you stop being a taxpayer because you change residence, all income pending allocation, meaning income not yet declared under the timing rules, is included in the base of the last period you must declare, with a supplementary return if needed, without penalty, late-payment interest or surcharge (article 14.3). If you move to another European Union State, you may instead declare it as it is obtained, also with a supplementary return for the last period.

The exit tax on shares

If you have been a Spanish income taxpayer for at least ten of the fifteen periods before the last one you must declare, leaving gives rise to a gain equal to the difference between the market value of your shares or holdings and their acquisition value when that market value exceeds 4,000,000 euros in total or, failing that, when on the accrual date of the last period you hold more than 25% of an entity and those holdings are worth more than 1,000,000 euros; in the second case, only for those holdings (article 95 bis.1). The gain is part of the savings income of the last period (article 95 bis.2).

It is not always paid on departure. If the move is a temporary work assignment to a country that is not a tax haven, or for another reason to a country with a tax treaty containing an exchange-of-information clause, payment can be deferred on request (article 95 bis.4). If you become a taxpayer again without having sold the holdings, you can claim a refund of what you paid (article 95 bis.5). And if you move to another European Union State, or a European Economic Area State with effective exchange of information, you may elect that the gain is only self-assessed if, within the following ten years, you sell the holdings, stop being resident in the EU or EEA, or fail to meet the notification obligation (article 95 bis.6), with conditions to be checked case by case; the Spanish Tax Agency has Modelo 113 for this notice.

If you move to a tax haven

A Spanish national who proves new tax residence in a country or territory considered a tax haven remains a Spanish income taxpayer in the year of the move and the following four years (article 8.2): the status is not lost, so those years are still declared here.

What you still declare in Spain

After you leave, what you still obtain in Spain, for example from a property, is taxed under Non-Resident Income Tax; we explain it in our Modelo 210 guide and in the guide to selling property as a non-resident.

Frequently asked questions

About leaving Spain

If I leave in July, do I only declare up to July?

Not necessarily. The tax period is the calendar year (article 12.1 of the Personal Income Tax Act) and residence is assessed over that year (article 9.1): if you are still resident that year, you declare the whole year’s income in Spain.

What is the exit tax?

When you leave, the difference between the market value and the acquisition value of your shares or holdings is treated as a gain if you have been a taxpayer for at least ten of the previous fifteen periods and that value exceeds 4,000,000 euros in total or, failing that, you hold more than 25% of an entity with holdings worth more than 1,000,000 euros (article 95 bis.1 of the Personal Income Tax Act). It can be deferred for temporary moves and, if you move to the EU or EEA, you may elect a specific regime (article 95 bis.4 and 6).

What happens to income that was pending?

It is included in the base of the last period you must declare, with a supplementary return if needed, without penalty, interest or surcharge. If you move to another European Union State, you may declare it as it is obtained (article 14.3 of the Personal Income Tax Act).

If I keep my flat in Spain, what do I declare?

What you still obtain in Spain from the property is taxed under Non-Resident Income Tax; we explain it in our Modelo 210 guide.

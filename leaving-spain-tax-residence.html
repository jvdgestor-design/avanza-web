Leaving Spain: what happens with Spanish tax when you stop being resident

Esta guía en español →

If you are leaving Spain to live in another country, your last year here and whatever you keep in Spain have their own tax rules. Grupo Avanza Consultores, tax and accounting adviser in Paterna (Valencia), checks in which year you stop being resident, prepares your last income tax return with what has to be included in it and, afterwards, the non-resident returns for what you still have here. The year you leave is not split

The Spanish income tax period is the calendar year (article 12.1 of the Personal Income Tax Act) and residence is assessed over that year (article 9.1). Someone who leaves mid-year can remain resident for the whole year, for example after spending more than 183 days here or if the core of their economic interests is here, and then declares worldwide income for the whole year in Spain. The date on the plane ticket does not decide this on its own.

What goes into your last return

When you stop being a taxpayer because you change residence, all income pending allocation is included in the base of the last period you must declare, with a supplementary return if needed, without penalty, late-payment interest or surcharge (article 14.3). If you move to another European Union State, you may instead declare it as it is obtained, also with a supplementary return for the last period.

The exit tax on shares

If you have been a Spanish income taxpayer for at least ten of the fifteen periods before the last one you must declare, leaving triggers a taxable gain equal to the difference between the market value of your shares or holdings and their acquisition value when that market value exceeds 4,000,000 euros in total, or when you hold more than 25% of an entity and those holdings are worth more than 1,000,000 euros; in the second case, only for those holdings (article 95 bis.1). The gain is part of the savings income of the last period (article 95 bis.2). For moves to another European Union or European Economic Area country, the Spanish Tax Agency has a specific notice, Modelo 113.

If you move to a tax haven

A Spanish national who proves new tax residence in a country or territory considered a tax haven remains a Spanish income taxpayer in the year of the move and the following four years (article 8.2).

What you still declare in Spain

After you leave, what you obtain in Spain as a non-resident, such as rent, imputed income from a property or its sale, is taxed under Non-Resident Income Tax, usually with Modelo 210.

What we do

Using your dates and ties, we check in which year you stop being resident, what goes into your last return and whether the exit tax applies, and we prepare that return. Afterwards we handle the non-resident returns for what you keep in Spain.

Official sources

Frequently asked questions

About leaving Spain

If I leave in July, do I only declare up to July?

Not necessarily. The tax period is the calendar year (article 12.1 of the Personal Income Tax Act) and residence is assessed over that year (article 9.1): if you are still resident that year, you declare the whole year’s income in Spain.

What is the exit tax?

When you leave, the difference between the market value and the acquisition value of your shares or holdings is taxed as a gain if you have been a taxpayer for at least ten of the previous fifteen periods and that value exceeds 4,000,000 euros in total, or you hold more than 25% of an entity with holdings worth more than 1,000,000 euros (article 95 bis.1 of the Personal Income Tax Act).

What happens to income that was pending?

It is included in the base of the last period you must declare, with a supplementary return if needed, without penalty, interest or surcharge. If you move to another European Union State, you may declare it as it is obtained (article 14.3 of the Personal Income Tax Act).

If I keep my flat in Spain, what do I declare?

As a non-resident, what you obtain in Spain, such as rent, imputed income from the property or its sale, is taxed under Non-Resident Income Tax, usually with Modelo 210.

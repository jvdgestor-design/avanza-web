Receiving a gift from family abroad while living in Valencia (Spain): gift tax, deadlines and requirements

Esta guía en español → · Эта страница на русском →

If you live in Spain and your parents or other relatives abroad give you money, or a flat they own in their country, it is you who pays Spanish gift tax, and in the Valencian Community the large reliefs only apply if the gift is documented correctly before the money moves. Who pays, and which tax

A gift is taxed on the person who receives it, not on the person who gives it (Law 29/1987 on Inheritance and Gift Tax, article 5.b). It falls under Inheritance and Gift Tax (Impuesto sobre Sucesiones y Donaciones), not income tax: what is subject to Inheritance and Gift Tax is not subject to personal income tax (Personal Income Tax Act, article 6.4). If the recipient is resident in Spain, which is decided under the income tax rules (Law 29/1987, article 6.2), the gift is taxed here in full, wherever the money or the property is and wherever the giver lives (article 6.1). Whether the money comes from Russia, Ukraine, the United Kingdom or anywhere else does not change who pays. It changes the evidence you need of where the money comes from.

Which region, and which rules

For a gift of money to someone resident in Spain, the tax belongs to the autonomous community (region) where the recipient resides, and that region's rules apply (Law 22/2009 on regional financing, article 32, paragraphs 2.c and 5). For this tax, you reside in the region where you have spent the most days of the five years before the gift, and you are presumed, unless shown otherwise, to be present where your main home is (article 28.1.1.º.b). If you arrived in Spain recently, or moved from another region, that count comes first, because the reliefs you can claim depend on it. The Basque Country and Navarre are different: there the tax is their own (foral) tax, and if the recipient is habitually resident there on the date of the gift, a gift of money belongs to their own tax authority (Law 12/2002, article 25.One.c, and Law 28/1990, article 31.1.b).

Living in the Valencian Community: the reliefs

Valencian law (Law 13/1997 of the Generalitat) gives two reliefs on gifts within the close family. First, a reduction of the taxable amount: 100,000 euros for children aged 21 or over, for the spouse and for parents; for children under 21, 100,000 euros plus 8,000 euros for each year they are short of 21, up to 156,000 euros; grandchildren follow the same scale by age, and grandparents get 100,000 euros. Adopted children and adoptive parents count as children and parents. For those limits, all gifts from the same giver in the previous five years are added together (article 10 bis.2.a). Second, a 99 % relief on the tax that remains, for gifts to the spouse, parents, children, grandchildren and grandparents (article 12 bis.1.b).

Brothers and sisters, uncles and aunts, nephews and nieces have no reduction for kinship, but they do have a relief of 25 % of the tax on gifts (article 12 bis.2.b, which refers to blood relatives of the second and third degree in the collateral line). Valencian Law 5/2026 has already set that relief at 50 % for gifts made from 1 June 2027 (its article 25). The conditions below apply to them too.

The conditions that are most often missed

The document. Both reliefs require the gift to be made in a public document, in practice a notarial deed (escritura pública), or to be formalised in that way within the filing period. For gifts of money that, adding up those from the same giver to the same recipient in the previous three years, do not exceed 4,000 euros or the equivalent in foreign currency, the deed can be replaced by documents signed digitally through a qualified trust service under Regulation (EU) 910/2014, unless the validity of the transaction requires otherwise. This is the wording introduced by Valencian Law 5/2026 (articles 21 and 22) for gifts made from 11 August 2026. If the money arrives in another currency, what counts for the limit is its value in euros.

The origin of the money. When money is given, the reduction and the relief only apply if the origin of the funds is properly evidenced and, in addition, the document states where the money comes from and identifies how it is delivered, for example the bank transfer (articles 10 bis.2 and 12 bis.4). A deed that only says that a sum is given does not qualify, even if it is signed before a notary, and the requirement also applies below 4,000 euros.

A complete return, on time. The 99 % relief only covers what is included in full in a self-assessment filed within the deadline, or filed late before the tax authority has made any request (article 12 bis.3).

The deadline: one month

The tax arises on the day the gift is made (Law 29/1987, article 24.2). If you live in the Valencian Community, you have one month from that day to file form 651 with the Valencian Tax Agency (Agència Tributària Valenciana) (Law 13/1997, article 12 ter.One.b). The State regulations give thirty working days from the day after the gift (Royal Decree 1629/1991, article 67.1.b); in the Valencian Community the one-month period of Valencian law is the one that counts, and in other regions their own law has to be checked. In practice, the document should be ready before the money is sent.

If the gift is a flat or house abroad

When parents living abroad give their child, who lives in Spain, a property located in another country, the child is still taxed here, because a Spanish resident is taxed wherever the asset is (Law 29/1987, article 6.1). What changes is who deals with it. The regional financing law only gives a region gifts of property located in its territory (Law 22/2009, article 32.2.b), so this gift is not filed with the Valencian Tax Agency but with the Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria). Even so, the child is entitled to apply the rules of the region where he or she resides (Law 29/1987, second additional provision, paragraph One.1.d): for a resident of the Valencian Community, the Valencian reduction and relief, with the same conditions, starting with the public document. Form 651 is filed with the Spanish Tax Agency within thirty working days from the day after the gift, according to the instructions for the form, which is the State period (Royal Decree 1629/1991, article 67.1.b), not the Valencian month.

Giving the flat is not the same as selling it and giving the money. If the parents sell the property in their country and give their child the price, the gift is a gift of money: it is dealt with by the region where the child lives, with that region's deadline and with the requirement to evidence the origin of the funds, which in that case is the sale itself. If the deed of gift is signed before a notary in another country, it is worth checking before signing that the document meets the public-document requirement, because the reduction and the relief depend on it.

If tax has already been paid abroad

A Spanish resident who pays a similar tax abroad on the same gift can deduct in Spain the lower of two amounts: the tax paid abroad, or the result of applying the average effective rate of the Spanish tax to the part taxed abroad (Law 29/1987, article 23.1).

What to prepare before the transfer

The order matters. First, establish the region where you are resident for this tax and what its law requires. Then prepare the evidence of where the money comes from in the giver's country: which account it leaves, where it comes from, and the documents that prove it, in practice translated into Spanish. With that, the public document stating the origin of the money and the means of delivery; then the transfer, which has to match what is written; and, within the deadline, the self-assessment. If both parents give, each one is a separate giver, and the limits of the reduction are counted per giver.

If you do not live in Spain

If the person receiving the gift is not resident in Spain, they are only taxed here on what is in Spain (Law 29/1987, article 7), and the case is dealt with, in general, by the Spanish Tax Agency. The logic is the same as for inheritances, which we explain in inheriting in Spain as a non-resident heir.

Frequently asked questions

About gifts from family abroad

Do I pay tax in Spain if my parents send me money from abroad?

If you are resident in Spain, there can be tax: the gift falls under Inheritance and Gift Tax and is paid by the person who receives it, wherever the money was (Law 29/1987, articles 5.b and 6.1). It is not declared in your income tax return (Personal Income Tax Act, article 6.4). How much is paid depends on the rules of your region, which in the Valencian Community has a reduction and a 99 % relief between parents and children, subject to conditions.

Which region's rules apply to a gift of money?

Those of the region where the recipient resides, meaning the one where he or she has spent the most days of the five years before the gift (Law 22/2009, articles 28.1.1.º.b and 32). In the Basque Country and Navarre the tax belongs to their own tax authorities. If the recipient is not resident in Spain, the case is dealt with, in general, by the Spanish Tax Agency.

What must the deed say in the Valencian Community?

That the gift is made and for how much, but also where the money comes from and how it is delivered, for example the bank transfer. Without that, and without the origin of the funds being properly evidenced, neither the reduction nor the 99 % relief apply (Law 13/1997, articles 10 bis.2 and 12 bis.4). Up to 4,000 euros or the equivalent in foreign currency, adding up gifts from the same giver to the same recipient in three years, the deed can be replaced by documents signed digitally through a qualified trust service, but the origin of the money is still required.

What if my parents give me a flat they own abroad?

If you are resident in Spain, you are taxed here on that gift, but it is dealt with by the Spanish Tax Agency, not by your region, because the regional financing law only gives a region gifts of property located in its territory (Law 22/2009, article 32.2.b). You are entitled to apply the rules of the region where you reside (Law 29/1987, second additional provision, paragraph One.1.d). Form 651 is filed within thirty working days from the day after the gift (Royal Decree 1629/1991, article 67.1.b).

What is the deadline to file?

If you live in the Valencian Community and the gift is money, one month from the date of the gift, on form 651 with the Valencian Tax Agency (Law 13/1997, article 12 ter). The State regulations give thirty working days from the day after the gift (Royal Decree 1629/1991, article 67.1.b), which is the period for gifts handled by the Spanish Tax Agency; in other regions their own law has to be checked.

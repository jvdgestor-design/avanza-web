Plusvalía municipal tax in Spain for non-residents: who pays on a sale or an inheritance

Ask about my caseWhatsApp us

If you sell urban property in Spain as a non-resident individual, the buyer files and pays the plusvalía municipal tax as your substitute and can then claim it back from you; if you inherit or receive a gift, you pay it yourself. The deadline is thirty working days for a sale or a gift and six months, extendable to one year, for an inheritance (articles 106 and 110 of the Spanish Local Finance Act). Who pays depends on how the property changes hands

Plusvalía municipal is the Spanish municipal tax on the increase in value of urban land (Impuesto sobre el Incremento de Valor de los Terrenos de Naturaleza Urbana). It taxes the increase in value of urban land that becomes apparent when ownership is transferred by any means, whether a sale, an inheritance or a gift. Land classed as rural for property tax (IBI) purposes is not subject to it (article 104, sections 1 and 2, of the consolidated text of the Local Finance Act, Royal Legislative Decree 2/2004).

On a sale, the taxpayer is the seller (article 106.1.b). If the seller is an individual who is not resident in Spain, the law makes the buyer the substitute taxpayer (article 106.2): the buyer must file and pay instead of the seller, and can then claim the amount paid back from the seller, unless the law provides otherwise (article 36.3 of the General Tax Act). The substitution only applies where the seller is an individual; if a non-resident company sells, the company itself remains the taxpayer. That is why, on a sale by a non-resident, it is advisable for buyer and seller to agree in the contract how plusvalía will be calculated and covered before signing the deed.

On an inheritance or a gift, the taxpayer is the person who receives the property, the heir or the recipient of the gift (article 106.1.a). There is no substitute here: an heir living outside Spain has to file the plusvalía return. On a gift, the donor must also notify the town hall of the transfer (article 110.6.a); on a sale, the buyer must do so (article 110.6.b).

Deadlines: thirty working days or six months

The tax arises on the date of the transfer (article 109.1.a). From that date, the return is filed with the relevant town hall within thirty working days for transfers between living persons, such as a sale or a gift, and within six months, extendable to one year at the taxpayer’s request, for transfers on death (article 110.2). The document recording the transfer is attached to the return (article 110.3). Each town hall decides in its by-law whether taxpayers self-assess and pay within those same deadlines or whether the town hall issues the assessment and notifies it with a payment deadline (article 110, sections 4 and 5). The notary must warn the parties of the filing deadline in the deed (article 110.7).

What decides the amount

The tax base is the value of the land for property tax (IBI) purposes (the cadastral value of the land) on the date the tax arises, multiplied by a coefficient that depends on the number of complete years of ownership, up to a maximum of twenty years; if the period is less than one year, the annual coefficient is prorated by complete months (article 107, sections 1, 2.a and 4). The town hall may apply a reducing coefficient of up to 15% to that value (article 107.2.a) and, after a general cadastral revaluation, a temporary reduction of the new values (article 107.3). Each town hall approves its coefficients within the legal maximums, which are updated by legislation; if a coefficient in the by-law exceeds a new legal maximum, the legal maximum applies (article 107.4). The consolidated text in the Spanish Official Gazette (BOE) records a change to those maximums taking effect on 1 December 2026 (article 10 of Royal Decree-law 29/2026; the consolidated BOE already records Parliament’s repeal of another decree-law on these coefficients, 26/2026, so we check its status in each case), so the coefficient is always checked for the date of the transfer rather than taken from an old table. The rate set by the town hall is then applied to the base, and it cannot exceed 30% (article 108.1).

If there was no gain, or a smaller one than the formula gives

If a comparison of the land value at acquisition and at transfer shows no increase, the transfer is not subject to the tax. To prove it, the higher of the value stated in the deed and any value verified by the tax authorities is taken on each date, without adding costs or taxes; for a property with land and a building, the land value is found by applying to those prices the share that the cadastral value of the land represents in the total cadastral value on the date the tax arises. If the acquisition or the transfer was an inheritance or a gift, the value declared for Inheritance and Gift Tax is taken instead of the value stated in the deed, compared with any value verified by the tax authorities, which prevails if higher. This is not automatic: the transfer must be declared and the acquisition and transfer deeds provided (article 104.5).

If there was an increase but the actual increase is lower than the base calculated with the cadastral value and coefficients, the actual increase is taken as the base at the taxpayer’s request (article 107.5). That is why we run both calculations before signing and the appropriate one is declared. If a transfer was not subject to the tax for lack of an increase, the period before that acquisition is not counted on the next transfer of the property, except for the non-taxable transfers in section 3 of article 104 and those under the second additional provision of Law 27/2014 on Corporate Income Tax (article 104.5, last paragraph).

If you inherit: a longer deadline and a possible relief

As well as the six-month deadline, which can be extended, municipal by-laws may provide a relief of up to 95% of the tax on inheritances passing to descendants and adopted children, spouses, and ascendants and adoptive parents (article 108.4). Whether it exists, and on what conditions, depends on the municipality, so we read the current by-law in each case. Inheritance Tax for an heir living abroad is a separate tax with its own deadline: see inheritance in Spain for non-resident heirs.

What plusvalía does not cover

Plusvalía municipal is a town hall tax. A sale by a non-resident also involves Non-Resident Income Tax on the gain, with the withholding paid by the buyer and the seller’s Form 210: see selling property in Spain as a non-resident and, from the buyer’s side, buying from a non-resident: Form 211.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about plusvalía for non-residents

Who pays plusvalía if the seller lives outside Spain?

If the seller is a non-resident individual, the buyer is the substitute taxpayer and files and pays instead of the seller (article 106.2 of the Local Finance Act), and can then claim the amount back from the seller unless the law provides otherwise (article 36.3 of the General Tax Act). The deadline is thirty working days from the sale (article 110.2.a).

Is plusvalía due if I sell for less than I paid?

If a comparison of the land value at purchase and at sale shows no increase, the sale is not subject to the tax, but it must still be declared with the acquisition and transfer deeds (article 104.5). Costs and taxes on the transactions are not added to those values.

I inherited a flat in Spain and live abroad. What is the plusvalía deadline?

Six months from the date the tax arises, extendable to one year if you request it (article 110.2.b). The heir is the taxpayer even when living abroad (article 106.1.a), and the municipal by-law may provide a relief of up to 95% for descendants, spouses and ascendants (article 108.4).

What rate applies to plusvalía municipal?

The rate set by each town hall, which cannot exceed 30% (article 108.1). It applies to the cadastral value of the land multiplied by the coefficient for the years of ownership, or to the actual increase if that is lower and the taxpayer asks for it (article 107, sections 1 and 5).

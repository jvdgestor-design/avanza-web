Spanish nationality by residence: what changes in your taxes

Español

Becoming a Spanish national does not, by itself, change your tax residence: Article 9 of the Spanish Personal Income Tax Act (IRPF) decides it, before and after the grant. One specific rule does change: Article 8(2) keeps a Spanish national who moves to a tax haven as an IRPF taxpayer in the year of the move and the following four years; current rules define non-cooperative jurisdictions (Law 36/2006, First Additional Provision, list set by ministerial order).

The procedure in brief

Nationality by residence under the Spanish Civil Code generally requires ten years of lawful, continuous residence immediately before the application. The period is five years for people who have obtained refugee status; two for nationals by origin of Ibero-American countries, Andorra, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea or Portugal, or for Sephardic Jews; and one in the specific cases in Article 22(2), such as one year of marriage to a Spanish citizen without legal or factual separation.

Good civic conduct and sufficient integration must also be shown, evidenced by the Spanish-language diploma (DELE) at A2 level or higher and the constitutional and sociocultural test (CCSE), save for the exemptions and dispensations in Order JUS/1625/2016. The maximum period for a decision is one year from receipt of the application by the Directorate-General named in the rules, and administrative silence is negative. Approval does not complete acquisition: it opens a 180-day period from the day after notification for the required declarations and the request for registration in the Civil Registry, which is what completes the acquisition of nationality.

Civil Code · Articles 21–23 · Order JUS/1625/2016 · Article 10 · Royal Decree 1004/2015 · Article 11(3) · Law 20/2011 · Article 68 · Justice Ministry · Nationality portal

Three tax points first

Nationality does not decide tax residence

Tax residence depends on your days in Spain and on where your economic interests are. Your passport plays no part in that test.

IRPF Act, Article 9(1).

Two passports do not decide where you pay tax

If two States each treat you as resident, the double taxation treaty between them decides, not your nationality.

IRPF Act, Article 9; applicable treaty.

What does change once you are Spanish

If you move to a tax haven, Article 8(2) keeps you in Spanish IRPF for five tax years; current rules define non-cooperative jurisdictions. As a foreign national, this rule did not apply to you.

IRPF Act, Article 8(2); Law 36/2006, First Additional Provision.

Your tax residence does not change on the day of the oath

The lawful residence the Civil Code requires for nationality and tax residence are different concepts. Tax residence is set by Article 9(1) of the IRPF Act using two tests: spending more than 183 days of the calendar year in Spain, with sporadic absences counted unless you prove tax residence in another country; or having your main centre or base of activities or economic interests in Spain. In addition, you are presumed resident, unless you prove otherwise, if your spouse who is not legally separated and your dependent minor children habitually reside in Spain.

That is why approval does not alter your position for that year: if you were already tax resident, you remain so under the same tests, and a Spanish national living abroad may not be tax resident in Spain.

One consequence is worth checking before you apply: the years you build up for nationality usually coincide with years of tax residence. In the years in which you were tax resident, you were taxed in the IRPF on your worldwide income, wherever it arose and wherever the payer was resident, and if you held assets or accounts abroad Form 720 may have applied. If a return is missing, the guide to regularising your taxes as a foreigner explains what decides the outcome.

IRPF Act · Article 9 · Article 2 · Tax residence when moving to Spain

Dual nationality: where you pay tax

Keeping your previous nationality, where that is possible, has no tax effect in itself: Spain applies Article 9, whether you hold one passport or two. If the other country also treats you as resident under its own law, there is no choice: the double taxation treaty between the two States applies, with its rules for allocating residence to only one of them. To prove to the other country that you are resident in Spain, you use the Spanish tax residence certificate.

What does change: moving to a tax haven

Article 8(2) of the IRPF Act provides that Spanish nationals who prove their new tax residence in a tax haven remain IRPF taxpayers in the tax year of the change and the following four. Article 8(2) speaks of a “tax haven”; current rules define non-cooperative jurisdictions in the First Additional Provision of Law 36/2006 (wording in force since 11 July 2021), with a list set by ministerial order.

The rule refers to Spanish nationals. While you were a foreign national, a move to a tax haven was judged under Article 9; the Article 8(2) rule did not reach you. Once you have acquired nationality, the same move keeps you in Spanish IRPF for five tax years.

IRPF Act · Article 8(2) · Law 36/2006 · First Additional Provision

If you later leave Spain

If you stop being a taxpayer because you change residence, income pending allocation is included in the tax base of the last period for which you must file an IRPF return. If the move is to another European Union State, you may opt to allocate it as it is obtained, through supplementary returns. The guide to leaving Spain and your taxes covers the other effects of the move.

IRPF Act · Article 14(3)

While you are resident: Renta, 720, family and the Beckham Law

As a resident you are taxed on your worldwide income. Whether you must file a Renta return is decided by Article 96: among other cases, someone whose only income is employment income up to 22,000 euros need not file (15,876 euros if there is more than one payer and the second and subsequent payers exceed 1,500 euros); someone registered as self-employed during the year, or entitled to the international double taxation deduction, must always file.

Accounts, securities, insurance and property held abroad fall within the information return under the Eighteenth Additional Provision of the General Tax Act, Form 720; virtual currencies held abroad by a custodian are reported separately, on Form 721. Thresholds and deadlines are in the Form 720 guide.

Joint taxation does not depend on nationality either: it is only available if all members of the family unit, on 31 December, are IRPF taxpayers. And the Beckham Law regime does not require any particular nationality; it requires not having been resident in the previous five tax periods and a move for one of the reasons in Article 93; if you were tax resident in any of those years, you do not meet that requirement.

IRPF Act · Article 96 · Articles 82 and 83 · Article 93 · General Tax Act · Eighteenth Additional Provision

Questions about nationality and tax

Does acquiring Spanish nationality make me tax resident in Spain?

Not because of nationality. Tax residence is decided by Article 9 of the IRPF Act: more than 183 days in Spain in the calendar year, or having your main centre or base of activities or economic interests here. A Spanish national living abroad may not be tax resident in Spain.

I have dual nationality: in which country do I pay tax?

Dual nationality does not decide it. Spain applies Article 9 of the IRPF Act; if the other country also treats you as resident under its law, the double taxation treaty between the two States applies to allocate residence to one of them.

If I am already Spanish and move to a tax haven, do I stop paying tax in Spain?

No. Article 8(2) of the IRPF Act keeps as a taxpayer, in the year of the move and the following four years, a Spanish national who proves new tax residence in a tax haven. The article speaks of a “tax haven”; current rules define non-cooperative jurisdictions (Law 36/2006, First Additional Provision, list set by ministerial order). This rule does not apply to people who are not Spanish nationals.

During my years of residence for nationality, should I have filed Renta and Form 720?

If you were tax resident in those years, you were taxed on your worldwide income and had to file if you exceeded the limits in Article 96 of the IRPF Act; if you held assets or accounts abroad, Form 720 may have applied. Outstanding returns can be regularised.

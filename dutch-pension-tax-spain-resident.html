Dutch pensions when you live in Spain: AOW, workplace and public service pensions

If you are tax resident in Spain under the Spain–Netherlands tax treaty, AOW and ordinary workplace pensions are generally taxable only in Spain. A pension for Dutch public service follows a different rule: the Netherlands may tax it and Spain gives exemption with progression. We check each payment before preparing your Spanish income tax return.

Start with tax residence and the type of pension

This guide covers ordinary Spanish income tax (IRPF) for someone resident in Spain under the treaty. If you moved during the year, both countries regard you as resident, or a special tax regime may apply, first check your tax residence for that year. A Spanish address or the bank account receiving your pension does not settle the treaty position on its own.

The starting point is the Spain–Netherlands treaty signed on 16 June 1971. The official Dutch treaty register records its entry into force on 20 September 1972. The rules below were checked on 13 November 2026.

AOW: taxable in Spain under article 23

AOW is the Dutch state old-age pension paid by SVB. For a Spanish treaty resident, it is taxable only in Spain under article 23, the provision for income not otherwise covered by the treaty. The Dutch Tax Administration's 2026 table for Spain, page 95, expressly lists AOW under this article.

AOW is not a pension for government employment simply because a public body pays it. Keep the SVB annual statement separate from statements for pensions built up through employment: they can follow different treaty provisions.

Workplace pensions for past employment: article 19

Under article 19, pensions and similar remuneration for past employment are taxable only in the State where the recipient is resident, subject to article 20.1. For an ordinary Dutch private-sector workplace pension received by a Spanish treaty resident, that means Spain.

Do not apply this conclusion to every product called a pension. An annuity, a lump sum or a mixed scheme needs its own documents and classification. The payer's name alone is not enough to determine whether a payment relates to public service.

Dutch public service pensions: articles 20 and 25

Article 20.1 covers pensions paid by a State, its political subdivisions or local authorities, directly or from funds they created, for services rendered to them. A qualifying Dutch public service pension may be taxed in the Netherlands. This provision does not contain an exception based on the pensioner's nationality.

There is a specific exception: pensions for services connected with an industrial or commercial activity of those public bodies follow the ordinary rules, including article 19 for pensions (article 20.2). If your career includes different employers or periods, obtain the service history and a breakdown before classifying the pension.

For a pension falling within article 20.1, article 25.3 provides Spanish exemption with progression. Spain exempts the pension itself. When you file a Spanish tax return, include the exempt pension when working out the rate on other income in the general tax base, as required by Spanish income tax law. The treaty's credit method in article 25.4 concerns specified dividends, interest and royalties; it is not a general tax credit for Dutch pensions.

Two common situations

AOW plus a private-sector workplace pension. For a Spanish treaty resident, AOW follows article 23 and the workplace pension follows article 19. Both are taxable in Spain. Reconcile each annual statement and any Dutch deductions separately.

AOW plus a qualifying public service pension. AOW remains taxable in Spain. The public service pension may be taxed in the Netherlands and receives Spanish exemption with progression. It can therefore affect the rate on your AOW and other income in the general tax base. These examples assume periodic payments and ordinary Spanish residence taxation.

If Dutch tax is still being withheld

A Dutch deduction does not by itself change which country has the treaty taxing right. Where Spain has the exclusive right, check the Dutch withholding exemption and, where appropriate, how to correct tax already withheld. NetherlandsWorldwide explains the withholding exemption procedure; do not assume that Spain will simply credit the deduction.

For AOW, SVB's exemption guidance asks residents of Spain for a Spanish Tax Agency certificate of tax residence for treaty purposes, described as “residencia fiscal en España convenio”. An exemption must be arranged through the applicable Dutch process; moving or receiving the pension in Spain does not arrange it automatically.

Separate income tax from other deductions. SVB distinguishes wage tax, social insurance and healthcare deductions, including contributions handled through CAK. A wage-tax exemption does not automatically remove a healthcare contribution, and these contributions are not automatically foreign income-tax payments eligible for a tax credit.

Your Spanish return and the documents to prepare

The Spanish Tax Agency's guidance on foreign pensions starts from worldwide income for Spanish residents, subject to the treaty, and generally treats pensions as employment income. The Spanish classification of a private arrangement must be checked against its terms.

Whether you must file depends on the tax year, the income, the payers and the other filing conditions. A lower filing threshold can apply if your pension payer has no legal duty to withhold Spanish income tax. The Tax Agency states that treaty-exempt pension income is not counted for that threshold, although an exempt public service pension must be included when calculating the rate on other income in the general tax base. We check the rules for the year being declared rather than applying a current-year figure to an earlier return.

For the review, prepare:

  • The tax year, moving dates and evidence relevant to tax residence.
  • SVB and each pension provider's annual statement (jaaropgave), showing gross payments and deductions separately.
  • The pension award or scheme documents and employment history, including any public service, private employment or mixed periods.
  • Dutch assessments, withholding exemptions or refund decisions, plus any Spanish returns and treaty residence certificates already issued.

Bank receipts help reconcile payments, but the net amount alone does not show the gross pension, the type of deduction or the correct treaty treatment.

Frequently asked questions

About Dutch pensions in Spain

Is AOW taxed in Spain or the Netherlands?

For a Spanish tax resident covered by the treaty, AOW is taxable only in Spain under article 23. The Dutch Tax Administration lists AOW under this article in its 2026 treaty table for Spain. It is not treated as a public service pension merely because SVB pays it.

Are Dutch workplace pensions taxable in Spain?

An ordinary pension for past private-sector employment is taxable only in Spain if you are resident here under the treaty, under article 19. A pension for public service needs a separate check under article 20, including the exception for industrial or commercial activities.

Does Spanish nationality change the rule for a Dutch public service pension?

Article 20.1 of the Spain–Netherlands treaty does not contain a nationality exception. Check whether the payment and the former services actually meet that article's conditions. Spain gives exemption with progression under article 25.3 for a qualifying pension.

Can I deduct Dutch tax from my Spanish tax bill?

Do not assume that a Dutch deduction creates a Spanish tax credit. If Spain has the exclusive taxing right, check the Dutch withholding exemption or correction procedure. For a qualifying Dutch public service pension, article 25.3 provides Spanish exemption with progression. Identify healthcare and social contributions separately from income tax.

Do I have to file a Spanish tax return?

That depends on the tax year, the amount and type of income, the payers and the other filing conditions. A foreign pension is not automatically exempt because tax was deducted abroad. A pension exempt under the treaty is not counted for the income threshold, according to the Spanish Tax Agency, but exemption with progression requires it to be included when calculating the rate on other income in the general tax base.

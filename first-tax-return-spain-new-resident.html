Your first Spanish tax return after moving to Spain

Español

Before accepting a draft return, check your tax-residence year and income from every country. If you have moved to Spain and still receive foreign salary, pensions or investment income, or own property abroad, Grupo Avanza Consultores helps you organise your first return. We work online from Paterna, Valencia, with agreed fees. English service is in writing.

1. Establish residence for the tax year

Start with the year in which the income arose. That is not necessarily the year in which you will file the return. A NIE or the use of a Spanish bank account does not determine tax residence. We review days spent in each country, the centre of economic activities or interests and the other article 9 criteria, including the family presumption, which can be rebutted.

Under ordinary Spanish domestic rules, the tax period is the calendar year. Arrival does not automatically split the year into resident and non-resident periods. If you are resident for the year, income before the move may need to be reviewed too. If another country also treats you as resident, check whether a tax treaty with Spain applies for that year. If it does, your home, family ties and other facts may help determine where you are resident under the treaty.

If you are not a Spanish tax resident, a review under Non-Resident Income Tax may be needed instead of an ordinary resident return. Read about tax residence when moving to Spain.

2. Check whether you must file

Tax residence and the obligation to file a return are different questions. Filing depends on the nature and amount of income and the statutory cases in article 96 for the particular year. For salary, the payers and their withholding obligations are among the relevant circumstances. A foreign pension or salary cannot be assessed solely against the headline threshold in a tax-season article.

Not receiving a draft, or seeing no income in the AEAT tax data, does not establish that you have no filing obligation. Other circumstances can make filing compulsory. For example, the law requires a return from people registered during the year as self-employed under the schemes it specifies. We check your position and the rules for the year rather than importing thresholds from another filing season.

3. Gather income and transaction records from every country

Under ordinary Spanish personal income tax, a resident’s income is examined regardless of where it arises or where the payer is resident. Your first return therefore needs more than a Spanish payslip: the year must be reconstructed using source documents from each country.

  • Employment and pensions: gross amounts, the periods they relate to and tax withheld.
  • Accounts and investments: interest, dividends, disposals and acquisition records, rather than just closing balances or money sent to Spain.
  • Property: ownership, use, rental income, expenses and purchases or sales, including property abroad.
  • Business activity: invoices, accounting records and returns connected with self-employment.

Income, assets and transfers between accounts must be distinguished. A bank transfer does not, by itself, identify the taxable income. Transaction dates and currencies must also be documented for the calculation.

If you receive both an Irish State Pension (Contributory) and a pension from a former employer, keep the award letter and annual statement for each. Tell us who employed you and who pays each pension. If one pension comes from Irish government service, your nationality also matters under the Spain–Ireland treaty.

4. Check the draft against your records

The draft return is informative and must be checked against your actual tax position. Foreign income may be missing or incomplete in the information available to the AEAT. Its absence does not make it exempt.

We compare the tax data with certificates and transaction records. If the draft does not reflect your circumstances, the correct return must be prepared rather than confirming it unchanged. Personal and family circumstances and the requirements for any relief claimed must also be checked. Where a material source document is missing, we explain which calculation cannot yet be finalised.

5. Check relief for tax paid abroad

Start with the country, tax year and type of income. If a tax treaty applies, check which country can tax the payment and whether the tax charged follows the treaty. Spanish law can also provide relief when there is no treaty.

For income or gains arising and taxed abroad, article 80 limits the Spanish credit to the lower of two amounts. The first is the comparable income tax actually paid abroad on that income. The second is the amount calculated by applying the relevant Spanish average effective tax rate to the part of the taxable base taxed abroad. If the source country withheld more than a treaty allows, you may need to claim the excess back there.

We need certificates and, where relevant, foreign returns, assessments and evidence of payment. If the foreign assessment is still open, we identify that outstanding point before settling the double-tax treatment.

If you have just moved to Spain and receive a Danish pension, the former Spain–Denmark income tax treaty ceased to apply on 1 January 2009. For your first return under the ordinary Spanish rules, keep the statement showing your gross pension alongside your Danish tax assessment and evidence of any income tax paid on the pension. These records let us check the Spanish credit under article 80.

6. Check special regimes and separate reporting duties

If you have elected the inbound-worker regime, we request the election, accreditation and dates before preparing an ordinary return. The regime has its own rules and, for current elections, uses form 151. Being in your first year or holding a visa does not prove that the regime applies. Read about the Beckham Law conditions and application.

An income-tax return does not replace potential information-reporting duties for foreign assets and rights. That review is scoped separately: having a foreign bank account is not enough, on its own, to conclude that form 720 must be filed. Read about foreign assets and rights.

What to prepare and how we start

  • The tax year, identification and the date of your move.
  • Travel calendar, housing and family circumstances, and tax-residence certificates.
  • Income and transaction certificates from every country, identifying the currency.
  • Foreign returns, assessments, withholding records and evidence of tax paid.
  • Spanish tax data and earlier returns relevant to the year.
  • Inbound-worker regime documentation, if applicable.

We first agree the documents, scope and fees. Filing dates must be checked against the official calendar for the relevant season, distinguishing the income year, the filing window and any earlier direct-debit deadline. If a year is already overdue, we review the appropriate correction or late filing rather than waiting for the next season.

Frequently asked questions

Do I declare only income received after arriving in Spain?

Moving to Spain does not automatically split the tax year. Check your residence and tax regime for the calendar year, including any treaty that applies.

What if my foreign salary is missing from the draft?

Check your payslips and year-end statement even if the salary is missing from the draft. Under the ordinary rules, Spanish residents must consider worldwide income and any applicable treaty.

Does every tax resident have to file a return?

No. Filing depends on the nature and amount of income, the statutory cases for the year and the taxpayer’s circumstances.

Can I deduct all the tax paid in another country?

The credit depends on the tax actually paid, the Spanish limits and any applicable treaty. If the other country withheld more than the treaty allows, you may need to claim the excess back there.

What do you need for an online first-return review?

Tell us the year, your arrival date and the countries where you receive income. We will identify the residence, income and tax records needed and agree the scope and fees before work starts.

Services

What brings people to us

People moving to or investing in Spain rarely need a single tax return. What they need is to resolve a specific situation. The first question is usually tax residence, because almost everything else depends on it.

Arriving in Spain

NIE, census registration with the Tax Agency (Modelo 036), a tax representative where the law requires one, setting up as self-employed, and the tax treatment of the digital nomad visa, which does not always match what the advertising promises. We look at your contract, your country of tax residence and the applicable tax treaty before giving an answer.

Property in Spain

Buying, owning, renting out and selling. A non-resident individual who owns a home in Spain has to file Modelo 210 even if the property is empty, because the law imputes an income to it. In 2026 the filing calendar changed (Orden HAC/623/2026): rental income with tax to pay moves to a single annual deadline in April of the following year. For 2026 itself, that deadline covers the whole year only if you file the income grouped; if you file each period separately, it covers only the last quarter, and the earlier quarters keep the old deadlines. Returns with nothing to pay follow their own rules. We check which applies to you. Modelo 210: deadlines and rules →

Inheritances and gifts

Inheriting in Spain when you live abroad, or receiving money from family outside Spain while living here. Which tax authority handles the case, national or regional, and which regional rules can be applied decide how much is paid, and that is what we look at first. Tax residence

183 days is not the only rule

Being tax resident in Spain does not depend on having a NIE or being registered on the town hall register (padrón). Under the Spanish Personal Income Tax Act (Ley 35/2006, article 9), you are resident if you spend more than 183 days in Spain in the calendar year, and sporadic absences count towards those days unless you prove tax residence in another country (for countries treated as tax havens, the Tax Agency may require proof of 183 days spent there). You are also resident if Spain is the main centre or base of your business activities or economic interests. And you are presumed resident, unless you prove otherwise, if your spouse, not legally separated, and your dependent minor children live habitually in Spain. If your other country also treats you as resident and there is a double taxation treaty, the treaty rules decide. Counting days and ignoring the rest is a frequent mistake.

If you rent out a property in Spain and live abroad

As a general rule, a non-resident pays 24% on the gross rental income, with no expenses deducted. If you are tax resident in another European Union country, or in a European Economic Area country with an effective exchange of tax information, the rate is 19% and you may deduct expenses, provided you can prove they are directly related to the income earned in Spain (Non-Residents Income Tax Act, Real Decreto Legislativo 5/2004, articles 24.6 and 25.1.a). Without supporting documents, the deduction cannot be applied. The United Kingdom, the United States and Switzerland are not in the European Union or the European Economic Area. And leaving the property empty or keeping it for your own use does not exempt you from filing: the law treats an imputed income from urban property not used for a business as income obtained in Spain (article 13.1.h).

Questions

What people ask us most

Can you handle my case if I live outside Spain?

Yes. Most of these matters are handled remotely: documents are sent by email and signed with a digital certificate or, where appropriate, by a representative under a power of attorney.

I have just moved to Valencia. Where do I start?

With your NIE and with an assessment of where you are likely to be tax resident this year. That assessment shapes most of the other registrations.

Do you work in English?

Yes, in writing: email, WhatsApp messages and documents. You can also send us a voice note in English and we reply in writing. Documents for the Spanish authorities are prepared in Spanish, and we explain them to you in English.

Do you only file the paperwork, or do you also advise?

Both. We prepare and file the paperwork, and we also decide what should be filed and how it affects your taxes, so you only explain your situation once, to one person.

Do I have to come to the office?

No. The work is done remotely, by email or WhatsApp. Meetings in person are by appointment only.

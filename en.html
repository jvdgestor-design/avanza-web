Property in Spain

Buying, owning, renting out and selling. For a non-resident individual, an urban property in Spain that is not used for an economic activity may give rise to imputed income even if it is empty or kept for personal use (Non-Resident Income Tax Act, articles 13.1.h and 24.5). We check whether this applies to your case. In 2026 the filing calendar changed (article 5 of Orden EHA/3316/2010, as amended by Orden HAC/623/2026, and the latter order's sole final provision): rental income with tax to pay moves to a single annual deadline in April of the following year. For 2026 itself, that deadline covers the whole year only if you file the income grouped; if you file each period separately, it covers only the last quarter, and the earlier quarters keep the old deadlines. Returns with nothing to pay follow their own rules. We check which applies to you. Modelo 210: deadlines and rules → Selling property in Spain → Buying property in Spain →

Tax residence

183 days is not the only rule

Being tax resident in Spain does not depend on having a NIE or being registered on the town hall register (padrón). Under the Spanish Personal Income Tax Act (Ley 35/2006, article 9), you are resident if you spend more than 183 days in Spain in the calendar year, and sporadic absences count towards those days unless you prove tax residence in another country (for non-cooperative jurisdictions, under the tenth additional provision of Law 36/2006, the Tax Agency may require proof of 183 days spent there). You are also resident if Spain is the main centre or base of your business activities or economic interests. And you are presumed resident, unless you prove otherwise, if your spouse, not legally separated, and your dependent minor children habitually reside in Spain under those same criteria. If your other country also treats you as resident and there is a double taxation treaty, the treaty rules decide. Counting days and ignoring the rest is a frequent mistake.

If you rent out a property in Spain and live abroad

As a general rule, a non-resident pays 24% on the gross rental income, with no expenses deducted. If you are tax resident in another European Union country, or in a European Economic Area country to which mutual assistance rules on the exchange of tax information apply, under the tenth additional provision of Law 36/2006, the rate is 19% and you may deduct expenses, provided you can prove that the expenses are directly related to the income earned in Spain and have a direct and inseparable economic link to the activity carried on in Spain (Non-Resident Income Tax Act, Real Decreto Legislativo 5/2004, articles 24.6 and 25.1.a). Without supporting documents, the deduction cannot be applied. The United Kingdom, the United States and Switzerland are not in the European Union or the European Economic Area.

Spanish inheritance tax when the heir lives in Spain

By Grupo Avanza Consultores

Español · English · Français · Deutsch · Русский

If you inherit while living in Spain, inheritance tax (Impuesto sobre Sucesiones) is generally collected by the region (comunidad autónoma) where the deceased lived, under that region’s rules, and it is filed within six months of the death; the six-month extension is only granted if it is requested within the first five months. If the deceased lived in the Valencian Community, children, grandchildren, spouses, parents and grandparents deduct 100,000 euros from the taxable base, or more if they are descendants under 21, and receive a 99% relief on the tax due on everything declared in full and on time.

Which region collects the tax, and why

Anyone resident in Spain pays Spanish inheritance tax on a worldwide basis (obligación personal): on everything they inherit, wherever it is, including a bank account or a house in another country (Article 6.1 of Law 29/1987). Residence is decided under the Spanish income tax rules (Article 6.2).

When the heir lives in Spain, the tax is assigned to the region where the deceased had their habitual residence on the date of death, that region’s rules apply, and that region manages the tax by delegation from the State (Article 32, sections 2.a and 5, and Article 54.1.b of Law 22/2009). What matters is where the deceased lived, not where the heir lives: if your father lived in Paterna and you live in Madrid, the Valencian rules apply and the Generalitat Valenciana manages the tax. The deceased is treated as resident in the region where they spent the most days in the five years before death, counting temporary absences; unless proven otherwise, where their main home was (Article 28.1.1.º.b). Someone who moved to the Valencian Community from another region two years ago may still be subject to the rules of that other region.

This guide covers the common regime. If there are assets or residence in the Basque Country or Navarre, the rules are different; we summarise them in our guide for heirs living outside Spain.

If the deceased lived outside Spain

This is the case of a foreigner living in Valencia who inherits from a parent who lived in their home country. The tax is only assigned to a region when the deceased lived in one (Article 32.2.a of Law 22/2009); otherwise it is not assigned to any region and the self-assessment is filed with the State tax administration (second additional provision, section Two, of Law 29/1987). Regional rules still count: the heir is entitled to apply the rules of the region where the greatest value of what the deceased left in Spain is located and, if nothing was left in Spain, the rules of the region where the heir lives apply (section One.1.a of that provision). For this rule, the heir lives in the region where they have spent the most days in the previous five years (section One.2).

Because a resident heir pays tax on everything, an inheritance abroad is also declared in Spain. If the other country has taxed it with a similar tax, Spain allows a deduction of the lower of two amounts: what was paid there, or the result of applying the Spanish effective average rate to those assets (Article 23.1 of Law 29/1987). It is therefore worth having the foreign assessment, and proof of payment, before filing here.

Valencian allowances: family relationship and the family home

When the Valencian rules apply, the taxable base is reduced according to the relationship: descendants under 21, 100,000 euros plus 8,000 for each year they are under 21, up to 156,000; descendants aged 21 or over, spouses and ascendants, 100,000 euros (Article 10.1.a of Valencian Law 13/1997). Heirs with a disability of 33% or more add 120,000 euros, and 240,000 if it is 65% or more, or a permanently recognised intellectual or mental disability of 33% or more (Article 10.1.b).

The deceased’s main home has its own reduction: 95% of its value, up to 150,000 euros per heir, for the spouse, ascendants and descendants, or for a relative in the collateral line over 65 who lived with the deceased for the two years before death. The condition is to keep the home for the five years after the death, unless the heir dies before then (Article 10.1.c). If it is sold earlier, the reduction is lost: a self-assessment must be filed within one month of that day, with the part of the tax that was not paid and late-payment interest (Article 12 ter.Two). For a sole-trader business, a professional practice or shares in a family company there are 99% reductions, with their own requirements (Article 10.1, letters d and e).

On the remaining tax due, the Valencian Community grants a 99% relief to descendants, spouses and ascendants, groups I and II (Article 12 bis.1.a), and to heirs with a disability of 65% or more, or a permanently recognised intellectual or mental disability of 33% or more (Article 12 bis.1.c). Brothers and sisters, nephews and nieces, and uncles and aunts by blood receive a 25% relief (Article 12 bis.2.a), which the law raises to 50% with effect from 1 June 2027.

The relief has a condition that decides many inheritances: it only covers assets included in full in a self-assessment filed on time, or filed late without a prior request from the tax administration (Article 12 bis.3). A forgotten bank account or a property that turns up after such a request is taxed without it. That is why the inventory is closed before filing, including what is abroad.

What value is declared

Each heir’s taxable base is the net value of what they receive: the value of the assets and rights minus deductible charges and debts (Article 9.1.a of Law 29/1987). Property is declared at its Cadastre reference value (valor de referencia) on the date of death; if the declared value is higher, the declared value is used, and if there is no reference value, the higher of the declared and market value (Article 9.3). The reference value can only be challenged by requesting rectification of the self-assessment or by appealing the assessment issued by the administration (Article 9.4). Other assets, at market value, or at the declared value if higher (Article 9.2). A life insurance policy taken out by the deceased is added to what the beneficiary inherits (Article 9.1.c).

That value also matters later: if you sell what you inherited one day, your acquisition value for income tax is the one resulting from the inheritance tax rules, without exceeding market value (Article 36 of Law 35/2006).

The deadline: six months from the death

The self-assessment is filed within six months of the death (Article 12 ter.One.a of Valencian Law 13/1997 and Article 67.1.a of the inheritance tax Regulation). An extension for another six months can be requested, but only within the first five months; it is requested by the heirs, executors or administrators of the estate, with the death certificate, the known heirs and their relationship, and the location and approximate value of the assets. If no reply is notified within one month, it is deemed granted, and the extension carries late-payment interest from the end of the six months until filing (Article 68 of the Regulation). Requested after the fifth month, it is not granted.

Municipal plusvalía on the property

If the inheritance includes urban property and the town council has established the municipal plusvalía, the tax on the increase in value of urban land, the heir also pays it (Articles 59.2, 104.1 and 106.1.a of the Local Finance Law). The declaration set out in the council’s bylaw is filed with the council within six months of the transfer on death, extendable to one year at the heir’s request (Articles 109.1.a, 110.1 and 110.2.b). It is a different tax and a different administration: the inheritance tax extension does not extend it.

There are two ways to calculate the base: the land value for property tax (IBI) multiplied by the coefficient for the years elapsed, or the actual increase if the heir shows that it is lower (Article 107, sections 1, 2.a and 5). If there has been no gain, nothing is paid; to check this in an inheritance, the value declared for inheritance tax is taken, where applicable, as the transfer value (Article 104.5). The change to the maximum coefficients of Article 107.4 introduced by Article 10 of Royal Decree-law 29/2026 takes effect from 1 December 2026, so the date of death matters. Each bylaw may grant a relief of up to 95% of the tax to descendants, spouses and ascendants (Article 108.4): whether it exists and its conditions depend on the council, and they are checked before filing.

What happens afterwards in your income tax

What you inherit is not declared in your income tax (IRPF): income subject to inheritance tax is not subject to income tax (Article 6.4 of Law 35/2006). Nor is there a capital gain for the deceased when their assets pass on death (Article 33.3.b). Their last return does exist: their tax period ends on the day of death (Article 13), and that obligation passes to the heirs, though not any penalties (Article 39.1 of the General Tax Law).

If you keep urban property that is not your main home, is not used for a business and is not let, you will have deemed income from it in your income tax return, under Article 85 of Law 35/2006 (wording in force on 18/11/2026; changes have been published with effect from 1 January 2027); if you let it, you declare the rent. And if you sell it, the gain is calculated with the inheritance value, as explained above.

The documents that delay an inheritance

What usually holds up an inheritance is not the calculation but the documents: the death certificate, the certificate of last wills, the will or the declaration of heirs, the reference value of each property, account balances on the date of death and any life insurance. If there are documents or assets abroad, such as a will made in another country or an account in the home country, it is worth requesting them early, because they usually need an apostille and a sworn translation, and the six-month deadline runs regardless.

If you receive a gift instead

A transfer during lifetime follows other rules: see gifts of money from family abroad. If the heir lives outside Spain, see inheritance for non-resident heirs.

Questions

Inheritance tax for heirs living in Spain

Where is inheritance tax filed if I live in Spain?

In the region where the deceased lived, which manages the tax and applies its own rules: what counts is where they lived, not where you live (Article 32, sections 2.a and 5, and Article 54.1.b of Law 22/2009). If the deceased lived in the Valencian Community, the Generalitat Valenciana manages it. If they lived outside Spain, the self-assessment is filed with the State tax administration (second additional provision of Law 29/1987).

How much do children pay on an inheritance in the Valencian Community?

Children aged 21 or over, spouses and parents deduct 100,000 euros from the taxable base, and children under 21 deduct 100,000 plus 8,000 for each year they are under 21, up to 156,000 (Article 10.1.a of Valencian Law 13/1997). On the remaining tax they receive a 99% relief (Article 12 bis.1.a), but only for assets declared in full on time, or late without a prior request from the tax administration (Article 12 bis.3).

What is the deadline, and can it be extended?

Six months from the death (Article 12 ter.One.a of Valencian Law 13/1997 and Article 67.1.a of the Regulation). The extension of another six months is only granted if requested within the first five months, and it carries late-payment interest (Article 68 of the Regulation). The municipal plusvalía has its own six-month deadline, extendable to one year, requested from the town council (Article 110.2.b of the Local Finance Law).

If the deceased lived abroad and I live in Valencia, which rules apply?

The self-assessment is filed with the State tax administration, but you are entitled to apply the rules of the region where the greatest value of what the deceased left in Spain is located; if nothing was left in Spain, the rules of the region where you live apply (second additional provision, section One.1.a, of Law 29/1987). As a Spanish resident you also declare what you inherit abroad, with a deduction for a similar tax paid there, within the limit of Article 23 of Law 29/1987.

Do I have to declare the inheritance in my income tax return?

No: what is subject to inheritance tax is not subject to income tax (Article 6.4 of Law 35/2006). What must be filed is the deceased’s last return, whose period ends on the day of death (Article 13), and, if you keep urban property that is not your main home, is not used for a business and is not let, the deemed income (Article 85).

Is the main home reduction lost if I sell it?

If you sell it within five years of the death, yes, unless the heir dies before then (Article 10.1.c of Valencian Law 13/1997). In that case a self-assessment must be filed within the month after the sale, with the part of the tax that was not paid and late-payment interest (Article 12 ter.Two).

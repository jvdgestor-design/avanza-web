Long-term residence in Spain: national or EU status

Español · Русский

Spanish national long-term residence and EU long-term residence are different authorisations. Your nationality, lawful residence, absences and current permit need to be checked before identifying the route. The validity of your physical TIE card is reviewed separately.

Does this guide cover your situation?

This guide concerns Spain’s general immigration routes for third-country nationals. It is not aimed at EU, EEA or Swiss citizens. Family members of those citizens or Spanish nationals need their applicable route identified first. Recognised international protection, temporary protection and EU Blue Card mobility need separate checks.

Three checks before preparing an application

Which authorisation applies

Spanish national and EU long-term residence allow indefinite residence and work in Spain, with different requirements. EU status requires checks on resources and health insurance.

General regime for third-country nationals. Royal Decree 1155/2024, Articles 175, 176, 182 and 183.

How your time in Spain is evidenced

The ordinary route starts from five years of lawful, continuous residence. Permits, dates, absences and study periods are reconstructed; not every stay counts as residence.

The calculation depends on the route. Articles 176(a) and 183.

Your current permit and card

Long-term authorisation and the physical TIE card are separate. The authorisation is indefinite, but the card must be renewed; absences and other grounds may affect the permit.

TIE validity and loss of status require separate checks. Articles 178, 185 and 201.

Spanish national and EU long-term residence: the differences

National status authorises indefinite residence and work in Spain. EU status also allows this and adds EU long-term resident status, subject to its conditions. For EU status, fixed and regular sufficient resources and health insurance are checked; those requirements should not simply be copied to the ordinary national route.

The EU label does not grant EU citizenship or automatic permission to settle or work in every other State. Spanish nationality has its own procedure and requirements. If you already hold EU long-term resident status from another country, a specific Spanish procedure needs to be examined.

Royal Decree 1155/2024 · Articles 175, 176, 179, 182 and 183 · Organic Law 4/2000 · Article 32 · Civil Code · Articles 21 and 22

Residence, absences and studies: reviewing the full history

Under the ordinary continuity rules for both routes, absences of up to six consecutive months are allowed, without exceeding ten months in total within the five years. For employment-related absences, the total limit is eighteen months, while the six-consecutive-month limit remains. Reasons and evidence are checked, including the legal qualification concerning irregular departures. Special cases must not be transferred between routes by analogy.

For EU long-term residence, certain periods of stay for studies, pupil mobility, volunteering or training activities count at 50%, provided you hold residence status in Spain when applying. This rule is not transferred to national long-term residence or nationality. Recognised international protection, a pending application and temporary protection are also different situations.

Absences affecting continuity before obtaining status and absences that may cause its later loss are separate issues. If you previously held long-term status or have spent extended periods abroad, your status and decisions are reviewed before identifying the next step.

Royal Decree 1155/2024 · Articles 176, 183 and 201

Preparing the application and separating it from TIE renewal

For the initial review, gather your passport, residence decisions, current and previous TIE cards, study dates and evidence of absences and their reasons. Resources and health cover are also reviewed for EU status. This is not a closed document list: the procedure and your circumstances determine what is needed.

The general filing window is the two months before the residence authorisation expires. Filing is also possible within the following three months, without prejudice to possible sanction proceedings. If you hold a valid residence authorisation and already meet Article 183 for national long-term residence, you can apply without waiting for that window (Article 184.2). For long-term EU residence, Article 177.2 provides the same exception when Article 176 is already satisfied. The authorisation, issue of a TIE and later card renewals are separate steps.

The TIE must be kept up to date. Failing to apply for card renewal within the deadline does not by itself end the authorisation, but it does not remove documentary duties or other possible grounds for loss of status. Age, the relevant stage and the applicable dates are reviewed.

Royal Decree 1155/2024 · Articles 177, 178, 184 and 185 · Interior Ministry · TIE

Questions about national and EU long-term residence

What is the difference between Spanish national and EU long-term residence?

Both allow indefinite residence and work in Spain within their respective rules. Their requirements differ, including resources and health insurance for EU status. EU status does not make you an EU citizen or remove the procedures required by another country.

How are the five years and absences from Spain counted?

The ordinary route starts with five years of lawful, continuous residence. Dates, authorisations and the reasons for each absence are checked under the relevant route. Qualifying continuity before applying and grounds for losing status afterwards are separate checks.

Do periods of stay for studies count?

For EU long-term residence, qualifying study stays and the other categories in Article 176.a count at 50%, provided you hold residence status in Spain when applying. This rule does not automatically carry over to Spanish national long-term residence or nationality.

Do I need an employment contract, resources and health insurance?

EU long-term residence requires sufficient fixed, regular resources and health insurance. An employment contract is not the only possible way to evidence resources. These requirements should not be copied as a general checklist for the ordinary Spanish national route.

When should a long-term residence application be submitted?

The general window is the two months before expiry; filing is also possible within the following three months, without prejudice to possible sanction proceedings. With a valid residence authorisation and the requirements of Article 183 already met, you can apply for national long-term residence without waiting for that window (Article 184.2). For long-term EU residence, the same exception requires compliance with Article 176 (Article 177.2). We review the documents before setting your filing date.

Is renewing the TIE the same as renewing long-term residence status?

The authorisation is indefinite, while the physical card has its own validity and renewal rules. Failing to request TIE renewal within the time limit does not by itself extinguish the authorisation; documentary duties and any separate grounds for loss still need to be checked and addressed.

Does a permit from another EU country let me work in Spain?

The exact type of permit and its issuing country must be identified first. Holders of EU long-term status from another state have a Spanish route with its own requirements; the foreign permit does not automatically authorise residence and work in Spain. Not every foreign permit is EU long-term status.

Is this the route for EU, EEA or Swiss citizens and their family members?

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens have a different regime from these long-term authorisations. For their family members and family members of Spanish citizens, the applicable route must first be identified; family ties alone do not determine it. Permanent residence under that regime is checked separately.
